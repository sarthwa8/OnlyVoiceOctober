# Voice Agent Reliability in 2026: What Works, What Fails, and What Is Legal

Research date: 2026-10-18. Most numbers below come from vendors (Hamming, Cekura, Retell, Sarvam, Truecaller, dialer companies), so treat them as directional. Academic benchmarks (tau-Voice, EVA-Bench, "Lost in Multi-Turn") are the most trustworthy sources here. Data older than 2025 is flagged.

## 1. Which call types reach high completion or containment in production, and which fail?

### Takeaway
Narrow calls that relay information or confirm one thing (confirm, remind, look up, answer yes/no, collect a few fields) contain well. Containment falls with every extra turn, every state change and every free-form identifier the caller has to supply (addresses, tracking numbers, dates). Open-ended support, troubleshooting and long multi-step tool use are still where agents fail. On realistic customer-service tasks, even the best voice agents in 2026 finish only about two-thirds of tasks on the first try.

### Cited Findings
- **USPS IVR containment by intent (Oct 2020 to May 2021; old data, pre-LLM IVR):**
  - Information-relay intents contain well: general inquiry 93%, passports 91%, post-office lookup 90%.
  - Intents that change state or need caller details do worse: package tracking 72%, international tracking 63%, redelivery 62%, hold mail 60%, change of address 56%.
  - Specialized request types fell below 5%.
  - Cekura's reading: intents that need a tracking number, address or date do worse because "each extra turn gives the caller another chance to drop off."
  - Source: [Cekura, Containment Rate](https://www.cekura.ai/blogs/containment-rate), citing a USPS OIG white paper from Sept 2021.
- **IRS phone lines, 2026:** automation answered 41% of filing-season calls, up from 34% in 2025, on about 28M calls. Average wait still rose from 3 to 8 minutes. Source: [Cekura](https://www.cekura.ai/blogs/containment-rate), citing GAO-26-109074.
- **Gartner self-service survey (Dec 2023, older):** 14% of issues fully resolve in self-service, rising to 36% for issues customers called "very simple." 45% of self-service users said the company did not understand their goal. Source: [Cekura](https://www.cekura.ai/blogs/containment-rate), citing Gartner (n=5,728).
- **Vendor containment ranges are unaudited:**
  - The commonly cited 70–90% containment range traces back to vendor blogs with no audited source ([Cekura](https://www.cekura.ai/blogs/containment-rate)).
  - Published containment figures run from 14% to 78%, measured on different denominators. The most common defect is counting hang-ups as "contained." A federal watchdog found exactly that in a 90M-call IVR. (Search summary of vendor benchmark roundups, e.g. [JustCall benchmarks 2026](https://justcall.io/blog/ai-voice-agent-benchmarks-2026.html) and [Cekura](https://www.cekura.ai/blogs/containment-rate).)
- **Vendor claims, unverified:** Replicant claims 40–65% production containment across appointment confirmations, billing and order status. PolyAI claims above 50% on multi-turn conversations. Prosper AI claims it resolves 60% of inbound calls end to end. These came from search summaries of vendor pages, and I did not open the primary pages ([Prosper AI](https://www.getprosper.ai/blog/healthcare-voice-ai-agents-guide)).
- **Appointment scheduling (Retell, vendor):** practices with an operational owner who reviews metrics weekly reach about 90% containment. Practices that deploy and walk away reach about 70%. No sample size is given ([Retell](https://www.retellai.com/blog/how-to-set-up-ai-patient-appointment-scheduling)).
- **Reminder and confirmation campaigns (Fini, vendor):** claimed to cut no-shows by 30–50% within 90 days, against a baseline no-show rate of 18–27% across healthcare, home services and B2C ([Fini](https://www.usefini.com/guides/ai-voice-platforms-appointment-confirmations-rescheduling-high-volume-support)).
- **tau-Voice benchmark (Sierra, arXiv March 2026, ICML 2026):** 278 policy-constrained customer-service tasks in retail, airline and telecom, scored against the final database state.
  - In the paper, voice agents reached 31–51% pass@1 on clean audio and 26–38% under realistic noise and accents.
  - A text agent (GPT-5 reasoning) reached 85% on the same tasks, so voice kept only 30–45% of text capability.
  - 79–90% of failures were attributed to agent behavior, not the simulator.
  - Source: [arXiv 2603.13686](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13686).
- **tau-Voice leaderboard update (Sierra blog, 2026):**
  - Pass@1 rose from 30% (OpenAI gpt-realtime-1.0, Aug 2025) to 67% (xAI grok-voice-think-fast-1.0, Apr 2026).
  - Voice now keeps about 79% of the text reasoning ceiling (about 85%).
  - The benchmark is English only, with accents generated by TTS.
  - Source: [Sierra blog](https://sierra.ai/blog/tau-voice-benchmarking-real-time-voice-agents-on-real-world-tasks).
- **EVA-Bench (arXiv May 2026):** 213 scenarios, 12 systems.
  - No system scored above 0.5 on both the accuracy and the experience pass@1 metric.
  - The median gap between pass@k and pass^k was 0.44, so systems that can succeed often fail to succeed consistently.
  - Accent and noise changed scores by up to 0.314.
  - Source: [arXiv 2605.13841](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.13841).
- **Bolna in production:** processes about 1M calls/month (YourStory) or 200k+ calls/day (TechBuzz); the figures are from different dates. 60–70% of volume is English or Hindi. Published deployments include recruitment screening (Awign built an agent in 48 hours; Hyreo runs 5,000+ calls/month). No containment numbers are published.
  - Sources: [TechBuzz](https://www.techbuzz.ai/articles/bolna-raises-6-3m-for-india-voice-ai-platform), [Bolna blog](https://www.bolna.ai/blog), [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/20/bolna-nabs-6-3-million-from-general-catalyst-for-its-india-focused-voice-orchestration-platform/).

### Inferences
- **Safe call types for a demo:**
  - Single-purpose outbound calls with one to three structured outputs: confirm, reschedule, yes/no, a rating from 1 to 5, a choice from a short list, or a callback slot.
  - Screening calls with a fixed question list. Recruitment screening is a proven Bolna use case.
  - Information-relay calls where the agent speaks and the user acknowledges.
- **Risky call types:**
  - Anything shaped like tau-bench: multi-step, policy-constrained, several tool calls that change state (refunds, changing bookings with policy checks, troubleshooting).
  - Even the best 2026 voice agents fail about one in three of these on the first try, and worse under noise.
- **Reporting metrics:** report resolution or task success with hang-ups excluded, not raw containment. Judges who know the field will distrust a "90% containment" claim.

### Gaps
- I found no independent, audited containment benchmark broken down by call type for LLM voice agents in 2025–2026. The USPS data is pre-LLM IVR.
- I found no published containment or completion rates for Bolna, Sarvam or other Indian deployments.
- I did not confirm the Replicant and PolyAI figures on their primary pages.

## 2. Documented failure modes

### Takeaway
The main failure modes are:
- latency spikes (P90 and P95 in the 3–5 s range)
- endpointing and turn-taking errors (cutting in on users, or long silences)
- ASR errors on entities, accents, noise and code-switching, which then cascade into wrong tool calls
- drift and "getting lost" in longer conversations
- unreliable voicemail detection

Most failures start in the audio and ASR layers or in agent policy, not in the speech output.

### Cited Findings
- **Latency in production (Hamming, analysis of 10K+ voice agents, 2025–2026, vendor data):**
  - P50 turn latency 1.5–1.7 s, P90 about 3 s, P95 3–5 s.
  - STT buffering, not the LLM, is the main cause about 60% of the time.
  - Source: [Hamming](https://hamming.ai/blog/7-voice-agent-asr-failure-modes-in-production) and its [monitoring guide](https://hamming.ai/resources/voice-agent-monitoring-platform-complete-guide).
- **Latency targets:**
  - Hamming rates P95 under 800 ms as "good" and over 1,500 ms as "critical" ([Hamming metrics guide](https://hamming.ai/resources/voice-agent-evaluation-metrics-guide)).
  - The human turn gap is about 200 ms; past about 500 ms people start to hear hesitation; about 1 s end to end is the practical ceiling ([AssemblyAI](https://www.assemblyai.com/blog/low-latency-voice-ai)).
  - Telephone deployments typically run 800–1,400 ms ([becloudsolutions](https://becloudsolutions.com/technology/ai-latency-what-is-latency-and-why-it-matters-for-voicebots-and-virtual-agents/)).
- **Peer-reviewed latency measurements:**
  - Voice-Light deployment: median VAD-to-first-audio 758 ms, range 528–1,652 ms ([arXiv 2609.20995](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.20995)).
  - ChatGPT Voice: about 1,370 ms average time to first audio ([TASTE2, arXiv 2609.08956](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.08956)).
  - EVA-Bench counts a turn as "late" at 2.75 s or more, or 4 s or more on tool-call turns ([EVA-Bench](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.13841)).
- **ASR failure modes in production (Hamming):** noise-driven omissions, substituted intents, formatting drift, truncation and endpointing errors, hallucinated content, accent and dialect variability, and silent regressions.
  - Endpointing errors make the agent "respond too early, treating partial utterances as complete," which forces users to repeat themselves.
  - Failures cascade: a telephony problem makes everything downstream irrelevant, and an audio problem means the LLM never gets good input.
  - Source: [Hamming](https://hamming.ai/blog/7-voice-agent-asr-failure-modes-in-production).
- **Effect of noise and accents on task success:** a drop of 5–13 points in pass@1 ([tau-Voice](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13686)). Every provider dropped from clean to realistic audio ([Sierra](https://sierra.ai/blog/tau-voice-benchmarking-real-time-voice-agents-on-real-world-tasks)).
- **Telephony audio is low fidelity:** tau-Voice runs over G.711 µ-law at 8 kHz in 200 ms chunks to simulate phone audio ([Sierra](https://sierra.ai/blog/tau-voice-benchmarking-real-time-voice-agents-on-real-world-tasks)).
- **Long or multi-turn degradation ("LLMs Get Lost in Multi-Turn Conversation," ICLR 2026):**
  - Average performance drop of 39% from single-turn to multi-turn across six tasks, over 200k+ simulated conversations.
  - The drop is mostly from unreliability rather than lost capability: models make assumptions early and "do not recover."
  - It shows up even in two-turn conversations and in frontier models.
  - This is text-only, but it applies directly to voice dialog.
  - Source: [arXiv 2505.06120](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.06120).
- **Voice-specific multi-turn penalty:** tau2-bench reports an 18–25 point pass@1 drop when agents must guide a user rather than act alone. Cited in DuplexWorld ([arXiv 2608.10716](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.10716)).
- **Inconsistency:** the EVA-Bench gap of 0.44 between pass@k and pass^k means repeated runs of the same scenario often disagree ([EVA-Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.13841)).
- **Hindi and Indic ASR:**
  - Saarika v2 (Sarvam's own docs, VISTAAR benchmark): WER 18.32% across 11 languages, English 8.26%, Hindi 11.81%, the other 9 languages 20.15% ([Sarvam docs](https://docs.sarvam.ai/api/getting-started/models/saarika)).
  - Saaras v3 (Sarvam): about 19% WER on IndicVoices across the top 10 languages ([Sarvam blog](https://www.sarvam.ai/blogs/asr)).
  - Independent Hindi results: Saaras v3 and Gemini 3 Flash at 14.5% WER, IndicConformer at 13.8% ([SraVaani, arXiv 2608.08235](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.08235v1)).
  - Vaani Hindi benchmark: Saaras v3 at 12.3% clean, rising to about 21.8% WER in the hardest noise category; Google Chirp 3 at 12.3% and Gemini-3.1-Pro at 11.7% on clean audio ([arXiv 2606.21408](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.21408)).
  - An independent GitHub benchmark, whose author runs Saaras v3 in production: Saaras v4 at about 23.8% versus v3 at about 21.0% on fresh 2026 Hindi held-out audio ([logatra-benchmark](https://github.com/Nemukai/logatra-benchmark)).
- **Hinglish code-switching:**
  - ASR shows a 30–50% relative WER increase on code-switched versus monolingual speech ([HiACC corpus paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12329218/)).
  - Code-switched words have a WER about 2.5–3 points higher than average in Hinglish medical conversations ([arXiv 2603.06373](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.06373)).
  - Gnani, a competitor, claims leading Indic models reach 11–14% WER on code-mixed audio; this is unverified ([Gnani](https://www.gnani.ai/resources/blogs/blog-code-switching-speech-recognition-hinglish-asr)).
- **Voicemail / answering-machine detection (AMD):**
  - Vendors claim 95–98% accuracy.
  - JustCall reports about 75% for a dialer that analyzes 4–5 s of audio.
  - Twilio warns that AMD "will not always return the right answer," for example with very short voicemail greetings.
  - Cekura cites a 0.3% false-positive rate on 77k calls, a single vendor's self-report on English-language calls.
  - Sources: [Murf](https://murf.ai/blog/voicemail-detection), [JustCall](https://justcall.io/blog/?p=41006), [Cekura](https://www.cekura.ai/discover/voicemail-detection-testing-for-voice-ai).
- **Entity capture (emails and alphanumerics):**
  - TTS blurs domain names together, and special characters (dot, hyphen, underscore) and the end of the address are common failure points.
  - Fixes: spell letter by letter, confirm the domain separately, and avoid filler words while the caller is spelling.
  - Sources: [Vapi docs](https://docs.vapi.ai/assistants/email-address-reading), [Famulor](https://www.famulor.io/blog/email-capture-via-ai-voice-agent-the-complete-guide-to-accurate-data-and-optimized-processes), [fonio](https://fonio-knowledge-base.help.usepylon.com/articles/9494322951-capturing-names-email-addresses-phone-numbers-correctly).

### Inferences
- In Hindi and Hinglish over phone audio, expect roughly 12–22% WER, depending on noise and code-mixing. That is fine for intent detection and yes/no answers, but unreliable for names, addresses and alphanumeric IDs. Agents should avoid collecting free-form entities by voice, or verify them with a read-back, DTMF or an SMS link.
- Keep calls short, with few turns. Both the multi-turn research and the USPS per-turn drop-off point the same way.
- Plan for a latency budget of about 1–1.5 s per turn on Indian PSTN. Use filler or backchannel audio during tool calls.
- Do not let a demo's outcome depend on voicemail detection. Treat a "no answer / voicemail" outcome as normal and follow up by SMS or WhatsApp.

### Gaps
- I found no independent latency benchmark specific to Indian telephony (Exotel, Plivo India routes) or to Bolna's pipeline.
- I found no published WER for Saarika or Saaras on a dedicated Hinglish code-mixed test set. Sarvam lists code-mixing as a feature without a code-mixed WER.
- I found no Deepgram or ElevenLabs Hindi WER figures in a head-to-head comparison.
- I found no quantitative data on IVR navigation by AI agents (outbound agents calling businesses that sit behind IVR menus).
- I could not read the EVA-Bench full-text tables (the PDF did not parse), so its per-failure-type breakdown is missing.

## 3. Benchmarks and eval tools: key numbers

### Takeaway
The main academic benchmarks are tau-Voice (Sierra), EVA-Bench, VAmoS Bench and DuplexWorld. All are English only. Commercial eval and monitoring tools (Hamming, Cekura, Coval) publish thresholds and guidance, not audited benchmarks. Indic ASR benchmarks (IndicVoices, VISTAAR, Vaani, SraVaani, Vimarsha) exist for ASR only. I found no Indic task-completion benchmark for voice agents.

### Cited Findings
- **tau-Voice / tau³-Bench** ([arXiv 2603.13686](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13686); [Sierra blog](https://sierra.ai/blog/tau-voice-benchmarking-real-time-voice-agents-on-real-world-tasks); [tau2-bench repo](https://github.com/sierra-research/tau2-bench); leaderboard at taubench.com):
  - 278 tasks in retail, airline and telecom.
  - Best voice pass@1 is 67% (grok-voice-think-fast-1.0, Apr 2026), against a text ceiling of about 85%.
  - The repo warns that results from versions below 1.0.1 are not comparable with later versions.
- **EVA-Bench** ([arXiv 2605.13841](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.13841)): 213 scenarios and 12 systems. No system scores above 0.5 on both accuracy and experience pass@1. The median pass@k–pass^k gap is 0.44, and accent or noise changes scores by up to 0.314.
- **Other 2026 voice-agent benchmarks:** VAmoS Bench ([arXiv 2607.27453](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.27453)) and DuplexWorld ([arXiv 2608.10716](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.10716)). I did not read either in detail.
- **Hamming** (vendor, 10K+ agents): P50/P90/P95 latency of 1.5–1.7 s / about 3 s / 3–5 s. Its published metric definitions and thresholds are in the [metrics guide](https://hamming.ai/resources/voice-agent-evaluation-metrics-guide).
- **Cekura:** publishes a containment explainer, voicemail-detection testing guidance and "Voice AI Orchestration Benchmarks" covering Retell, Vapi, Pipecat, LiveKit and others ([Cekura](https://www.cekura.ai/discover/voicemail-detection-testing-for-voice-ai)). I did not read the orchestration-benchmark numbers.
- **Indic ASR benchmarks:**
  - Sarvam Saarika on VISTAAR: 18.32% WER across 11 languages ([docs](https://docs.sarvam.ai/api/getting-started/models/saarika)).
  - Saaras v3 on IndicVoices: about 19% ([Sarvam](https://www.sarvam.ai/blogs/asr)).
  - Vaani Hindi ([arXiv 2606.21408](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.21408)), SraVaani ([arXiv 2608.08235](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.08235v1)), Indic DiarBench ([arXiv 2607.23808](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.23808)) and Vimarsha ([arXiv 2609.24199](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.24199)).

### Inferences
- A hackathon team can borrow the evaluation method from tau-Voice and EVA-Bench: score the final database or JSON state, run each scenario several times (pass^k), and test with noise and accents. Showing a small eval harness with simulated Hinglish callers would look credible to judges, since no Indic agent benchmark exists.

### Gaps
- I did not read Coval's or Vapi/Retell's own reports.
- I found no Bolna-published benchmark.
- I did not check tau-Voice clean-versus-realistic scores per provider (they are only in charts).

## 4. What people answer and hang up on: pickup and completion for AI outbound calls

### Takeaway
Cold outbound calls get low answer rates in the US (about 5–15%) and are under heavy spam pressure in India. Truecaller users ignore about 80% of calls from India's designated commercial and service number series. Expected calls, such as warm leads called within a minute or reminders for something the person booked, do much better (vendors report 40–70%). The evidence is vendor-grade, not controlled, but every source points the same way.

### Cited Findings
- **US cold calling:** connection rates for AI outbound to cold lists are about 8–15%, similar to human cold calling. Warm lists (form fills, recent engagement) reach 40–70% when the AI calls within 60 s of the trigger. Vendor data ([Brilo AI](https://www.brilo.ai/resources/best-voice-ai-for-outbound-sales-calls)).
  - With clean, trusted numbers, cold-call answer rates are about 5–15% ([Leads at Scale](https://leadsatscale.com/insights/cold-calls-flagged-as-spam-how-to-fix)).
  - A brand-new number can fall below 10% within a week ([Layer3 Labs](https://www.layer3labs.io/guides/ai-outbound-calling-for-small-business)).
- **Spam labels:** answer rates fall 40–60% once a number is labelled "Spam Likely" (vendor claim). Pew (2025, cited secondhand) found about 80% of US adults ignore unknown callers, rising to about 95% when a spam label is visible ([PanTerra](https://blog.panterranetworks.com/insights/answer-rates-outbound-phone-number-reputation)).
- **What triggers spam flags:** volume, velocity, call duration patterns, complaint rates, attestation level and number history. Mitigations: ramp new numbers over 7–14 days and use local caller ID ([Thoughtly](https://thoughtly.com/blog/branded-calling-spam-label-prevention-ai-voice-agents)).
- **Connect rates (unsourced vendor figure):** connect rates reportedly fell to 2.3% in 2025 from 4.82% the year before ([JustCall](https://justcall.io/blog/sms-call-workflows-increase-connect-rates.html)).
- **Pre-call SMS:** Nooks, an AI dialer, says callback answer rates "nearly doubled" after adding SMS alerts that identify the caller. No baseline or sample size is given ([Nooks](https://www.nooks.ai/blog-posts/be-ready-for-every-callback-with-instant-sms-notifications)).
- **India, Truecaller data:**
  - Over eight months, Truecaller users ignored 81% of calls from the 140x promotional series and 79% from the 1600 service series.
  - Users manually blocked 74M calls from these series.
  - Daily blocks against 1600 numbers more than tripled since Oct 2025.
  - Source: [TechCrunch, Jul 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/08/truecaller-clashes-with-indias-telecom-regulator-over-anti-spam-rules/).
- **India spam volume:** Indians received about 4,168 crore spam calls in 2025, and Truecaller users blocked 1,189 crore. This is Truecaller's own report ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/amp/india/story/indians-received-over-4000-crore-spam-calls-in-2025-1189-crore-blocked-report-515765-2026-02-11); [FoneArena](https://www.fonearena.com/blog/475283/india-received-4168-crore-spam-calls-2025-truecaller.html)).
- **AI calls are still rare:** AI voice agents placed 4.0 of every 10,000 calls in Sept 2026 in NextPhone's dataset, and 8.2% of all calls were spam ([NextPhone](https://www.getnextphone.com/reports/ai-receptionist-call-report-2026)).

### Inferences
- Best-performing pattern: a call the user expects, started by the user ("call me now," a booked slot, a form submit), placed within seconds to minutes, and announced beforehand by SMS or WhatsApp.
- In India, even legitimate calls from 1600 numbers are ignored about 79% of the time by Truecaller users. A pilot that depends on cold pickup will look bad. For the demo, use opt-in flows: a missed-call trigger, a WhatsApp "call me" button, a QR code, or the user scheduling a callback.

### Gaps
- I found no controlled study of answer rates for expected versus cold AI calls.
- I found no Indian data on pickup rates for AI calls specifically, or for collections and reminder calls.
- The Pew 80%/95% and Google 31% figures are cited secondhand. I did not verify the originals.

## 5. Regulation affecting outbound AI calls: India and US

### Takeaway
- **India:** commercial calls must come from designated number series. Promotional calls use 140x, which DND preferences can block. BFSI and government service and transactional calls use 1600x. Utilities and logistics get 1601 (Phase 1, Aug 2026). Ordinary 10-digit numbers are barred for telemarketing, with penalties up to a one-year disconnection and blacklisting. A Sept 2026 TRAI rule reportedly treats all software-initiated calls (robocalls, AI) as A2P traffic that must be pre-declared to the operator.
- **DPDP Rules 2025:** notified 14 Nov 2025. The main consent and notice duties apply from about May 2027, and consent managers from about Nov 2026.
- **US:** the FCC's Feb 2024 ruling puts AI voices under the TCPA's "artificial or prerecorded voice" rules, which require prior express consent (written consent for marketing).
- **For a hackathon:** call only teammates or volunteers who opt in, with clear AI disclosure. Do not cold-call.

### Cited Findings
- **TCCCPR amendment, 12 Feb 2025:**
  - Telemarketing from ordinary 10-digit numbers is barred. Promotional calls move to 140; service and transactional calls move to 1600.
  - First violation: 15-day bar on outgoing services. Later violations: disconnection of all telecom resources, including PRI/SIP trunks, across all operators for one year, plus blacklisting.
  - Complaint threshold: 5 complaints within 10 days.
  - Sources: [TRAI PR No. 11 of 2025](https://cms.trai.gov.in/sites/default/files/2025-02/PR_No.11of2025.pdf); [FoneArena](https://www.fonearena.com/blog/446114/trai-tcccpr-2025-tougher-rules-spam-calls-sms.html); [trak.in](https://trak.in/stories/10-digit-phone-numbers-banned-for-telemarketing-calls-by-trai/).
  - One secondary source says consent for ongoing transactions is valid for 7 days and inferred consent lasts for the contract duration; this is unverified ([indialaw.in](https://www.indialaw.in/blog/civil/trais-combat-spam-protect-consumers/)).
  - Enforcement so far: 1,150+ entities blacklisted and 18.8 lakh resources disconnected ([MediaNama, Mar 2025](https://www.medianama.com/2025/03/223-1150-entities-blacklisted-18-8-lakh-numbers-disconnected-after-spam-regulation-amendments/)).
- **TRAI clarification, July 2026:**
  - 1600 is reserved for service and transactional calls from RBI, SEBI, IRDAI and PFRDA-regulated entities to existing customers, plus government-to-citizen calls. These cannot be tagged, blocked or filtered under TCCCPR.
  - 140 is for promotional calls from any sector, and can be blocked only through the customer's DND sector preferences.
  - Source: [MediaNama, Jul 2026](https://www.medianama.com/2026/07/223-trai-releases-clarification-designated-promotional-transactional-number-series/).
  - Separately, a 19 Nov 2025 direction reportedly requires RBI-regulated banks and NBFCs to send OTP and EMI reminder calls from 1601 numbers; 485 entities had moved to the 1600 series by Nov 2025 ([Frejun](https://frejun.com/series-call-rules-restrictions-india/)). This may conflict with the next item.
- **1601 series, Aug 2026:** a new series for service and transactional calls from utilities (power, water, city gas, LPG) and logistics and courier firms, barred from promotional use.
  - Allocated directly to eligible entities, not to aggregators. Operator onboarding within 90 days.
  - Source: [MediaNama, Aug 2026](https://www.medianama.com/2026/08/223-trai-1601-series-logistics-courier-services/).
- **TRAI rule on robocalls and AI calls, about 18 Sept 2026:**
  - All software-initiated communications, including robocalls and prerecorded or AI voices, are classed as A2P.
  - Businesses must pre-declare these numbers to operators; calls from undeclared numbers are classified as spam.
  - Operators may charge up to 5 paise/min termination on A2P calls.
  - The article does not mention AI-disclosure or consent rules.
  - Source: [Nokiamob, 19 Sept 2026](https://nokiamob.net/2026/09/19/indias-telecom-regulator-targets-ai-robocalls-and-caller-id-apps/). This is secondary; I did not find the primary TRAI text.
- **TRAI versus caller-ID apps:** TRAI sought IT Act powers against Truecaller, Hiya and Whoscall for labelling 140 and 1600 numbers as spam ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/08/truecaller-clashes-with-indias-telecom-regulator-over-anti-spam-rules/)).
- **DPDP Rules 2025:** notified 13 or 14 Nov 2025 (sources differ).
  - Phase 1: Data Protection Board provisions, effective immediately.
  - Phase 2, about Nov 2026: consent manager registration, minimum net worth Rs 2 crore.
  - Phase 3, about May 2027: notice, consent, security safeguards, breach notification, data-principal rights, retention and erasure, and children's data.
  - The IT SPDI Rules 2011 continue in the meantime.
  - Sources: [compliancehub](https://compliancehub.wiki/india-dpdp-consent-manager-november-2026-phase-two-deadline-compliance/); [Lidings](https://www.lidings.com/media/legalupdates/DPDPA/); [DSK Legal](https://dsklegal.com/?p=26455).
- **US, FCC declaratory ruling (8 Feb 2024, unanimous, effective immediately):**
  - AI-generated or cloned voices count as "artificial or prerecorded voice" under the TCPA.
  - Callers need prior express consent, or prior express written consent for telemarketing, unless the call is for an emergency or exempt.
  - Calls must include identification and disclosure and offer an opt-out.
  - State AGs and individuals can sue.
  - Sources: [Hunton](https://huntonak.com/privacy-and-information-security-law/fcc-issues-declaratory-ruling-that-tcpa-applies-to-ai-generated-voice-calls); [BHFS](https://www.bhfs.com/insight/fcc-declares-ai-generated-calls-subject-to-tcpa/).
  - Commentators note the ruling clarified existing rules rather than creating new ones ([Womble Bond Dickinson](https://www.womblebonddickinson.com/us/insights/blogs/fcc-adopts-declaratory-ruling-confirming-ai-voice-calls-are-subject-tcpa-takes)).
- **Competitor critique of Bolna:** a competitor says Bolna's site publishes nothing on DPDP, TRAI DND scrubbing, DLT templates or the RBI Fair Practices Code. This is unverified and comes from a competitor ([Caller Digital](https://www.caller.digital/blog/best-ai-calling-platform-india-2026-comparison)).

### Inferences
- **Practical setup for a hackathon pilot:**
  - Call only consenting participants: teammates, judges or volunteers who enter their own number or trigger the call.
  - Disclose "this is an AI assistant calling on behalf of X" at the start.
  - Offer an opt-out ("say stop / press 9").
  - Do not cold-call purchased lists. Don't make promotional calls from regular 10-digit numbers.
  - Collect minimal personal data and state the purpose. This is cheap DPDP-aligned practice even before Phase 3 applies.
  - Inbound calls, or outbound calls the user starts ("call me back"), avoid most telemarketing exposure.
- **Pitch framing for production:** commercial deployment would need 140/1600/1601 numbering, A2P declaration, DND scrubbing for promotional use, and DPDP notice and consent by May 2027. Saying this in the pitch shows judges the team understands the rules.

### Gaps

- It is unclear whether non-BFSI, non-utility businesses (clinics, D2C, edtech) making service calls must use 1600 or 1601, or can still use other numbers.
- Sources conflict on whether 1601 is a BFSI sub-series (Frejun) or the utilities and logistics series (MediaNama).
- I found no explicit Indian rule requiring AI-voice disclosure.
- I did not confirm FCC or TCPA developments after 2024 (for example, later rulemaking on AI disclosure).

## 6. Design patterns that make narrow voice agents robust

### Takeaway
Robust 2026 agents share these patterns:
- narrow scope and short calls with few turns
- a structured schema filled by post-call extraction
- read-back confirmation for every captured entity
- DTMF or SMS/WhatsApp links for sensitive or alphanumeric data
- deterministic fallbacks (human handoff, SMS follow-up, callback)
- latency masking during tool calls
- continuous eval against simulated noisy, accented callers with pass^k consistency checks

### Cited Findings
- **Confirmation read-back for entities:** spell letter by letter, confirm the domain separately, clarify one character rather than lose the lead, and don't use filler words while the caller spells ([Famulor](https://www.famulor.io/blog/email-capture-via-ai-voice-agent-the-complete-guide-to-accurate-data-and-optimized-processes); [Vapi docs](https://docs.vapi.ai/assistants/email-address-reading)).
- **Fallbacks when voice capture fails:** DTMF, SMS links or alternate contact methods ([Famulor](https://www.famulor.io/blog/email-capture-via-ai-voice-agent-the-complete-guide-to-accurate-data-and-optimized-processes)).
- **Short, few-turn conversations:** multi-turn LLM performance drops by 39% on average, and models that go wrong early do not recover ([arXiv 2505.06120](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.06120)). USPS data shows lower containment for intents needing more caller-supplied fields ([Cekura](https://www.cekura.ai/blogs/containment-rate)).
- **Monitoring:** alert on drops in intent recognition, rising fallback or clarification rates, rising interruption frequency, and latency spikes after prompt changes. Track P90 and P95, not only P50 ([Hamming](https://hamming.ai/blog/7-voice-agent-asr-failure-modes-in-production)).
- **Operational ownership:** weekly metric review is associated with about 90% versus about 70% containment in appointment scheduling (vendor claim, [Retell](https://www.retellai.com/blog/how-to-set-up-ai-patient-appointment-scheduling)).
- **Eval method:**
  - Score the final state rather than the transcript, and test under realistic audio ([tau-Voice](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13686)).
  - Measure pass^k for consistency and add accent and noise perturbations ([EVA-Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.13841)).
- **Containment is not resolution:** report resolution separately and don't count hang-ups as contained ([Cekura](https://www.cekura.ai/blogs/containment-rate)).
- **Voicemail:** measure AMD false positives and false negatives separately on your own traffic. Short greetings fool detectors ([Murf](https://murf.ai/blog/voicemail-detection); [Cekura](https://www.cekura.ai/discover/voicemail-detection-testing-for-voice-ai)).
- **Number reputation:** ramp volume over 7–14 days and watch for sudden drops in answer rate ([Thoughtly](https://thoughtly.com/blog/branded-calling-spam-label-prevention-ai-voice-agents)).

### Inferences
- **Recommended demo architecture:**
  1. A user-triggered or consented call.
  2. A greeting with AI disclosure.
  3. Three to five closed questions in Hinglish, using yes/no, numbers or short-list choices. Use DTMF for numeric IDs.
  4. A read-back summary and a confirmation.
  5. LLM extraction into a JSON schema after the call, with confidence flags.
  6. An SMS or WhatsApp summary with a correction link.
  7. A dashboard showing outcomes plus a "needs human" queue.
- Following this design keeps each call inside the regime where 2026 agents are reliable. It also produces visible, structured output that judges can check.

### Gaps
- I found no controlled studies quantifying the lift from read-back confirmation, DTMF entry or pre-call SMS. All the evidence is vendor guidance or anecdote.
- I found no data on optimal call length for completion. The direction (shorter is better) is supported, but I found no curve of completion rate against duration.
