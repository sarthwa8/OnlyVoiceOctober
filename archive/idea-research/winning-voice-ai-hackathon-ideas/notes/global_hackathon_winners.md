# Winning Projects at Global Voice-AI Hackathons, 2024–2026

Research date: 2026-10-18. Every winner below has a source URL. Where a placing or date came only from a search snippet and not from the page itself, it is marked "(snippet)". Abbreviations in the catalogue: **Dir** = who the call is with (OUT = the agent calls out to a third party; IN = the user talks to the agent, by phone or in an app; M2M = agent talks to agent). **Mkt** = B2B or consumer (B2C). **Scope** = narrow/scripted task vs open-ended conversation.

## Which projects won hackathons run or sponsored by Vapi, Retell, ElevenLabs, Deepgram, LiveKit, Cartesia, Daily/Pipecat, Twilio, Hume, OpenAI, Google Gemini Live, a16z, YC, AGI House, Cerebral Valley, college hackathons, and Devpost challenges?

### Takeaway
More than 70 sourced winners were found across about 15 events. The best-documented are the ElevenLabs x a16z Worldwide Hackathon (Feb 2025, 300+ projects, 9 cities), Google's Gemini Live Agent Challenge (results May 2026, 1,536 submissions), Cartesia's October 2024 series, Daily/Pipecat's Open Source Realtime AI Hackathon (Oct 2024), and the AssemblyAI/LiveKit/Rime SF Voice Agent Hackathon (Sept 2025). Official winner lists could not be retrieved for YC-hosted voice hackathons (Oct 2025, Apr/May/June 2026), Retell, Hume (2024–26), Deepgram, LiveKit-only, or Hack the North/TreeHacks voice prizes.

### Inferences
- Of the roughly 73 winners, about 9 have the agent **calling out to real third parties** (GibberLink, Procuro, Dealwise, Two Somethin', Claimsio, Clinicians Unchained, Call My Parts, Deals Machine, and arguably gyanta). That is a small share, but they are over-represented at the top: global grand prize, SF 1st, NYC 2nd with judges "blown away", and the "most mind-blowing" award. The demo of an AI phoning a real business live seems to be the strongest single "wow" moment.
- Most winners are **inbound/in-app, consumer, and multimodal** (voice plus camera, screen, map, or robot). The phone-line "user calls a hotline" format is rare among winners.
- B2B winners are almost always **narrow and vertical**: supply-chain parts, airspace, debt collection, prescription denials, surgery, hiring roleplay, podcast ads.

- Most winner posts do not describe the demo itself.

## What did the winners have in common?

### Takeaway
Winners pair voice with **real-world action** (phoning someone, controlling a device or app, ordering via a tool) or with **another modality** (vision, maps, robots). They target a **specific user in a specific moment**, often hands-busy (surgery, driving, cooking, physio, drone). The demo is live and visceral, and often has humour or emotion. Judges reward demos they can touch themselves.

### Cited Findings
- **Live real-world calls create the wow moment.** Procuro's demo placed calls to suppliers and judges were "blown away". GibberLink's winning demo was itself a phone call between two agents. — [ElevenLabs](https://elevenlabs.io/blog/announcing-the-winners-of-the-elevenlabs-worldwide-hackathon)
- **Hands-on judging.** At AI Week Milan, judges entered their own phone numbers and took live calls; the winner says that "won them over" and that the app had to be fully functional for judges to test it. — [Resonance Substack](https://resonancevoiceai.substack.com/p/going-it-alone-i-built-a-hackathon)
- **"It just works."** Voxy's Best Overall was justified with "The platform just works", and the judge cited accessibility for non-technical businesses. — [AssemblyAI](https://www.assemblyai.com/blog/voice-agent-hackathon-sept-19)
- **Hands-busy and multimodal contexts.** Google's own summary of Gemini Live winners: voice-first and often hands-free (surgery, driving, desktop, drone); voice combined with vision (webcam, screen, pointing); agents that act beyond chat; emotional or personal context. — [Google Cloud](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/developers-practitioners/winners-and-highlights-of-the-gemini-live-agent-challenge)
- **Integration breadth.** Cruzy won for its "seamless combination of video, voice AI, and map navigation" ([Daily](https://www.daily.co/blog/open-source-realtime-ai-hackathon-winner-announcement/)). Stella was noted for its "impressive integration of multiple AI services" ([Cartesia](https://www.cartesia.ai/blog/hacktober)).
- **Humour and fun get dedicated prizes.** AssemblyAI SF split "Most Fun" three ways (cooking bot, podcast, "Work Daddy"). Cartesia gave a notable mention to Roasted.lol and placed a "foul-mouthed" robot chef 2nd. Vapi placed an "escape awkward situations" app 2nd. — [AssemblyAI](https://www.assemblyai.com/blog/voice-agent-hackathon-sept-19); [Cartesia](https://www.cartesia.ai/blog/hacktober); [Vapi X](https://x.com/Vapi_AI/status/1796598283688870209)
- **Emotional, health, and accessibility themes recur:** PT coach (Pep), memory loss (Memento), stuttering (Shiprail), blind users (6Sense), emotional companion (JohnKeats.AI), cardiac-arrest recorder (Code Blue). — sources in catalogue above
- **Technical depth gets a named prize.** Podweaver's 250 ms timing precision with cloned voices impressed technical judges. — [AssemblyAI](https://www.assemblyai.com/blog/voice-agent-hackathon-sept-19)
- **Roleplay / practice simulators** win repeatedly: PersuAIsion, SheBeTalking, AI✧Debate, Talvin Job Tryouts. — catalogue sources

### Inferences
- The recurring winning pattern is "voice plus doing something judges can see happen" (a phone call is answered, a drone moves, an order is placed, a form fills in). Pure chat-with-a-voice rarely wins top prizes.
- Narrow, scripted call goals (get a quote, find a part, learn why a script was denied) are easier to demo reliably than open-ended conversation, and they still produced top prizes.

### Gaps
- Few sources describe stage mechanics (e.g., whether the phone was on speaker on stage) beyond Procuro and Deals Machine.

## Which hackathon winners went on to raise money or join YC?

### Takeaway
Hard evidence is thin. No catalogued winner has a verified, documented funding round or YC batch that clearly traces to the hackathon win. The visible "after" outcomes are virality (GibberLink), existing companies using wins as marketing (Talvin), and a promotional Forbes story about a YC hackathon win (April).

## What do judges and organisers say they look for?

### Takeaway
Stated criteria are consistent: working code over mockups, real-world or business value, originality, presentation, and how essential voice is to the experience. Implicitly, judges also look for latency, reliability, and a demo they can try themselves.

### Cited Findings
- AssemblyAI x lablab Voice Agent Hackathon (Sept 2026) rubric: **Application of Technology, Presentation, Business Value, Originality**. — [lablab.ai](https://lablab.ai/ai-hackathons/assemblyai-voice-agent-hackathon)
- UC Berkeley AI Hackathon 2026, "Best Use of Deepgram": judges weigh **creativity of the voice interaction, technical execution, and how essential voice is to the experience** (snippet). — [Devpost](https://ai-hackathon-2026.devpost.com/)
- An OpenAI Realtime voice hackathon judged on **running code (no mockups), innovation, real-world impact, theme alignment** (snippet). — [Justin Nathaniel](https://www.justinnathaniel.com/home/openai-realtime-voice-x-reasoning-hackathon)
- Gemini Live award names show the axes: multimodal integration and UX, technical execution and agent architecture, innovation and thought leadership. — [Google Cloud](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/developers-practitioners/winners-and-highlights-of-the-gemini-live-agent-challenge)
- The Out of Pocket health hackathon award categories include "most practical" ("I'm using this tomorrow"), "Most Fun", and "most mind-blowing". — [Out of Pocket](https://www.outofpocket.health/p/oops-2025-healthcare-ai-hackathon-projects)
- AgentPhone's YC hackathon brief: "Build agents that actually touch the real world across voice, SMS, iMessage… No more sandbox demos." Its brief included agents that haggle, book, and buy on your behalf (snippet). — [David Lieb X](https://x.com/dflieb/status/2052146123079639398)
- The YC Voice Agents hackathon kit tells teams to run simulated conversations, score transcripts, and fix failures before demoing. Judges want to "showcase interesting projects rather than just pick winners" (snippet). — [Pipecat GitHub](https://github.com/pipecat-ai/yc-voice-agents-hackathon)
- A winner's lessons: latency and accuracy both matter (slow suggestions break the call; wrong ones break trust). Polish matters because "anyone can vibe-code". Explain the idea clearly. — [Resonance Substack](https://resonancevoiceai.substack.com/p/going-it-alone-i-built-a-hackathon)
- A judge's essay: present in your own voice and on camera rather than with an AI voiceover, and don't let AI generate the core idea (snippet). — [HackerNoon](https://hackernoon.com/from-the-judges-seat-standing-out-in-hackathons-in-the-age-of-ai)
- AssemblyAI DEV shortlist: projects stood out for "innovation, usability, and thoughtful implementation of voice agents." — [AssemblyAI](https://www.assemblyai.com/blog/these-7-voice-ai-projects-just-blew-us-away)

### Inferences
- "How essential is voice?" is the key filter. Projects where a text box would do just as well are weak. Hands-busy, eyes-busy, or phone-native situations pass it easily.

### Gaps
- No full public scoring rubric was found from ElevenLabs, Vapi, Retell, YC, or Cartesia.

## Which ideas are now overdone?

### Takeaway
No organiser explicitly publishes a "banned ideas" list. Gallery composition shows heavy saturation in language tutors, AI companions/therapists, interview and pitch practice, travel planners, meeting assistants, and "agent calls businesses for quotes/bookings". These can still win, but only with a sharp twist or a very narrow user.

### Cited Findings
- A 2026 judging guide says the generic "AI chatbot for X" is no longer winning (snippet). — [Reskilll](https://blogs.reskilll.com/hackathon-ideas-that-won-2026-judges-actually-pick-examples/)
- The OpenAI Voice Hack Night (May 2026) gallery of 59 projects includes multiple near-duplicates: language tutor (KT languages), reading/phonics tutors (Phonex, Raven), a physics tutor (Curo), pitch/presentation coach (Athena), AI therapist (The Shrine), "AI that calls you as a friend" (Miraii), live translation x3 (Language Stream, Meta Glasses, Nekko), standup assistant (Scriber), hold-waiting phone agent (Alva Health), workout coach, and two surgeon voice-control projects. — [CV gallery](https://cerebralvalley.ai/e/openai-voice-hack-night/hackathon/gallery)
- The Cartesia Feb 2026 gallery (27 projects) shows the same clustering: two podcast-ad-insertion projects (Baksell, Addy), a standup assistant, VoicePal ("calls local businesses for pricing, availability, and bookings"), "Delegate" (completes real-world tasks on your behalf), festival/trip planner, and a scene-partner coach. — [CV gallery](https://cerebralvalley.ai/e/cartesia-voice-agents-hackathon/hackathon/gallery)
- "Calls local businesses for quotes" already won SF 1st in Feb 2025 (Dealwise). By 2026 it reappears as a non-winning entry (VoicePal, Voice AGI at a General Compute hackathon). — [ElevenLabs](https://elevenlabs.io/blog/announcing-the-winners-of-the-elevenlabs-worldwide-hackathon); [Voice AGI GitHub](https://github.com/ali-amjad52114/voice-agi)
- Travel companions won repeatedly in 2024–25 (Hugo, Voyagr, Cruzy, Ryoko, Roadmate), and so did podcast-ad insertion (Pochinki Feb 2025, Podweaver Sept 2025). Both are now common. — catalogue sources
- A lablab ideas guide lists a language-learning partner and a grief-support companion as stock ideas, and notes that wellness tools need disclaimers. — [lablab.ai](https://lablab.ai/guide/ai-hackathon-project-ideas)

### Inferences
- Likely overdone by late 2026: generic language tutor, AI therapist/companion, interview/pitch coach, travel planner, meeting/standup bot, podcast-ad insertion, generic "call restaurants/businesses to book or quote", and no-code "build your own voice agent" platforms.
- Still-differentiated angles from winners: a narrow B2B outbound call with a measurable outcome (Procuro, Clinicians Unchained, Call My Parts), voice in hands-busy professional settings (surgery, cardiac arrest, aviation), and a memorable, theatrical demo concept (GibberLink).

### Gaps
- No primary organiser statement explicitly names restaurant booking, AI therapist, or language tutor as "overdone". The inference comes from gallery saturation, not from judges' statements.
